How to read this, and what it can't tell you

  • Votes per capita = votes cast on the measure (yes + no) divided by the state's total resident population that year. It is not turnout among eligible voters (children and non-citizens are in the denominator), and it mostly reflects which election it was: about 96% of the top-decile measures are in presidential years. Use the other two sorts to compare measures on equal footing.
  • Engagement vs. the same ballot = votes on this measure ÷ votes on the most-voted measure on that state-year's ballot (ballots with at least 5 measures). Beyond ballot position removes the small trend with where the measure sits on the list.
  • Margin = (yes − no) ÷ (yes + no), in points. Blank ballots are not in the source counts. Hawaii treats blank ballots as "no" for passage, so a measure can have a positive margin here and still fail there.
  • A "ballot" is a state-year, which can combine a primary or special election with the general election; about 6% of ballots show vote counts that differ by more than 2× between measures, a sign of mixed electorates. That adds noise to the engagement measures.
  • Not shown: California (no vote counts yet) and 26 measures whose parsed counts disagreed with the stated yes percentage. Data: Wikipedia state lists; Census population estimates. All associations, not causes.
